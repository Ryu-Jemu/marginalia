import N from '../../components/note/N.astro';

The premise going in was that surveillance density explains crime. The data does
not support it.

Five variables across Seoul's twenty-five districts explain about 84% of the
variation between them.<N id="crime-model" /> The two that carry it are
population and the density of entertainment venues. CCTV count is not
significant.<N id="crime-significant" /> Crime concentrates where people and
nightlife concentrate.

Population, income and streetlight counts move together across districts, so
variance inflation was checked for every variable, and one of the five rests on
weaker data than the others.<N id="crime-streetlight" /> The fit was
cross-checked against cross-validated Lasso and Ridge; the same variables
survived with the same explanatory power.

Residuals are mapped by district rather than predictions, which names the
districts the model gets wrong. They cluster in a way that points at a
floating-population variable that was not available at district level.

The map over-predicts Seongdong by 1,862 crimes and under-predicts Mapo by
1,387,<N id="crime-residuals" /> and the districts it misses are the ones with a
daytime population the model never sees.
